Two worked fine-mapping examples

These examples show how to read the posterior, stopping evidence and sensitivity together. The stable example has persistent leading credible sets but a prior-sensitive background. The problem example reaches its initial stopping condition, then finds a better solution through refinement.

The baseline and refined fits use prusie 0.2.3rc6; additional sensitivity fits use 0.2.4.dev1. Values are rounded for display; linked tables retain full precision and variant IDs. See reproducibility for the input and software identities.

Stable signals with a prior-sensitive background

The baseline stops after three sweeps with the requested and effective ELBO rule, at tolerance 0.001. It has two active components and two singleton CS, {syn0100} and {syn0350}. Their posterior masses are approximately 0.9697 and 0.99994, and their effective numbers of variants are 1.146 and 1.001. Singleton purity is 1 by definition; it does not describe LD to surrounding variants.

Refinement tests two exclusions in one round, using four inner fits. Neither refit started from an excluded-variant solution improves the ELBO by more than 0.001, so no candidate is accepted. Before and after have the same ELBO, approximately −1363.854754, and identical PIPs. The refinement guide defines the ELBO used in this comparison. This supports stability under the executed search, without establishing global optimality.

syn0143 5.318e-11 0.2544

Increasing L from 5 to 6 leaves all PIPs unchanged. Changing reported CS coverage from 0.95 to 0.90 also leaves them unchanged, because that operation reuses the posterior. Fixing scaled_prior_variance=0.05 with estimate_prior_variance=False preserves both leading CS but changes the background substantially.

The component table explains why these are distinct observations. The estimated baseline retains two active components; the fixed positive-variance setting retains all five, three without retained CS. Disabling estimation removes the step that could shrink unused component V to zero, so those extra components remain available to distribute probability. The rise at syn0143 and syn0145 is evidence of sensitivity to this model choice, not proof of additional causal signals. The parameter is scaled prior variance; working-scale V also depends on the response variance.

All 500 variants are present in every setting. The LD diagnostic assesses all 500 and flags none, with no conditional-only or unassessed rows. That recorded result does not remove the prior sensitivity above.

A stopped fit improved by refinement

The 200-variant baseline satisfies its ELBO stopping rule after ten sweeps. That is evidence about one trajectory. Refinement evaluates five exclusion attempts in two rounds, using ten inner fits, and accepts one candidate. The returned candidate satisfies its inner stopping rule after three sweeps.

The ELBO changes from −70832.271605 to −70814.813295, a gain of 17.4583. The final logged sweep ELBO has a slightly different value and is not substituted in this comparison. Different starting solutions have different outcomes even though both satisfy their inner stopping rules.

The initial singleton CS {35} and {32} are replaced by {33}; the {86,90} CS remains. The stored refined PIPs at 32 and 35 are zero, and the stored value at 33 is one. These are observed numerical values, not missing variants; bounds mark initial probabilities that are close to, but below, one. The change in singleton support is substantial, rather than a redistribution within one unchanged CS.

What remains unresolved

The refined {86,90} CS has mass greater than 0.9999, approximately 1.595 effective variants, and |r| = 0.9989. Its roughly 0.177/0.823 probability allocation leaves LD-limited resolution. The LD panel displays r² (approximately 0.9978 off the diagonal) on a fixed 0–1 scale. The report shows this pair separately from the concentrated singleton {33}.

Variant 33 has genotype correlation about 0.532 with 90. That correlation is not a test of whether the two modeled effects are conditionally distinguishable. Neither the retained pair nor the number of CS determines the true number of causal effects. The result supports two distinct posterior summaries: a concentrated candidate at 33 and unresolved allocation within 86/90.

Sensitivity beyond the displayed candidates

L = 12 retains the initial three CS. Its largest PIP change over the full 200-variant support occurs at variant 0, outside the main candidate view: 0.03856 → 0.04825, an absolute change of 0.009694. The complete table and summary retain this change. Nine active components without retained CS contribute to the L = 12 background; their broad overlaps are kept in the component evidence without treating them as identified signal splits or merges.

All 200 variants are observed in every setting. All 200 receive a completed LD assessment, with none flagged. Variant 33 has the largest absolute conditional residual (about 3.872) but log LR about −90.95. Variant 0 has log LR about 3.560, but |adjusted z| about 0.644. Neither satisfies the joint rule log LR > 2 and |adjusted z| > 2. A residual label is therefore not an allele-flip claim. The LD matrix is close to singular; use the saved diagnostic warnings and input identity when assessing this result.

Read the full comparison, posterior and search summary and LD diagnostics. Paired R flip-flag agreement has not been established for these two examples.

Choose a signal detail

Select a component according to the question it answers. This illustrative fragment requires the problem fit's posterior summary and aligned LD matrix R; they are not the quick-start toy result. Its two retained CS justify distinct detail views:

from prusie.viz import plot_signal

singleton = plot_signal(summary, variant_id="33", R=R,
                        layout="compact", ld_measure="r2")
pair = plot_signal(summary, variant_id="90", R=R,
                   layout="compact", ld_measure="r2")

Here summary and R are the problem result and aligned LD matrix. IDs in this example identify its saved variants; use your own CS members or component IDs for another fit. See the API for local style, full layouts and exported plotting data, and the sensitivity guide for variant alignment and prior policies.

Sources and reproducibility

Inputs, licenses, numerical provenance and rendering commands

The stable inputs are independently generated synthetic data; their generation metadata and GPL-3.0-or-later notice ship with the package. The problem fixture uses a simulated response with real-genotype-derived sufficient statistics, without individual genotypes; source metadata records the pinned upstream input and BSD-3-Clause license, with upstream attribution.

The baseline and refined fits were produced with prusie 0.2.3rc6, and the additional sensitivity fits with 0.2.4.dev1. The stable provenance and problem provenance identify the inputs, source revisions and software versions. JSON and CSV downloads retain full precision. These inputs have no gene annotations.

To regenerate the figures and reports from the saved posterior and diagnostics:

python examples/post_analysis.py --saved-dir docs/examples/post_analysis \
  --output-dir output/rendered-examples --case both

The saved result directories include baseline, refined and alternative fits.

To fit both examples anew from the source checkout with the analysis extra installed:

python examples/post_analysis.py --output-dir output/post-analysis --case both

This explicit command runs fits and diagnostics with the installed solver. Opening the saved reports above does not. Each report includes PNG/SVG, complete CSV tables, lossless JSON/NPZ, provenance and a figure index. The compact main figures are supplemented by a separate variance-details figure and full search tables.
