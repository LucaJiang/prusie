Numerical accuracy

Numerical comparisons assess how closely prusie's fitted SuSiE posteriors agree with susieR when both implementations receive the same inputs and fitting settings. This page compares prusie 0.3.0 with susieR 0.16.6, using the complete alpha matrix of single-effect posterior inclusion probabilities as the primary agreement criterion.

The evidence includes fixed-reference checks, fresh susieR fits for FinnGen type 2 diabetes GWAS loci and GTEx Whole_Blood eQTL analyses, and an independent toy example. Results apply to the tested configurations; numerical agreement is separate from the statistical calibration of variant inclusion probabilities and credible sets.

Component posterior validation

Comparisons use susieR 0.16.6, commit 8e56a8e038e989856d106d9ca5175cc664fea9d2, with matched inputs, model settings and verified variant order. The numerical criterion is a maximum absolute difference of 1e-5, with rtol=0, across the complete final alpha matrix. Both matrices must have the same shape and retain their original component rows and variant columns, including inactive components and components without credible sets. Every entry must be finite and in [0,1][0,1][0,1], and each row must sum to one within 1e-10.

Marginal posterior inclusion probabilities (PIPs), credible-set memberships, posterior moments, variances, Bayes factors, ELBOs, iteration counts and convergence flags are additional diagnostics. Small differences near component activity or credible-set thresholds can change the reported summaries even when alpha passes. The fitting thresholds and stopping tolerances stay at the specified values. See Computational compatibility for the floating-point assumptions and full criterion.

The fixed-reference validation covers 184 RSS cases, each tested with n_threads=1, 2, 4 and 8, and with both overwrite_ld=False and overwrite_ld=True. All 1,472 case–setting comparisons pass, with a maximum absolute alpha difference from the fixed R reference of 9.65×10−89.65\times10^{-8}9.65×10−8. Each setting is fitted twice from independently reloaded inputs, giving 2,944 prusie calls. Repeated calls and the saved same-setting prusie comparison arrays agree bit for bit in alpha. The machine-readable summary records the settings, errors and identities of these separate R and prusie references.

These fixed-reference checks use refine=False and are separate from the fresh-R real-data comparisons and independent toy example. The results cover the tested inputs, model settings and platforms; refinement and stochastic draws across languages require separate validation.

Iterations and alpha differences

The IBSS sweep repeatedly updates each component's posterior and residuals, so prusie and susieR accumulate separate floating-point regroupings over successive sweeps. More sweeps therefore give more opportunity for small differences to appear, and both backends must stop before the comparison is anchored. Points below plot each case's maximum absolute alpha difference against the maximum of its two per-backend median IBSS iteration counts; cases where both backends reach max_iter=100 without convergence are highlighted.

Among cases where at least one backend converged, errors stay well below the 1e-5 acceptance limit on both panels. GWAS maximum within-case alpha differences range from 2.65×10−102.65\times10^{-10}2.65×10−10 (median across 9 one-to-five-sweep cases) to 4.05×10−84.05\times10^{-8}4.05×10−8 (maximum across 144 six-to-twenty-sweep cases), with the across-case maximum 4.05×10−84.05\times10^{-8}4.05×10−8. eQTL converged-pair errors behave similarly: across-case maximum 2.55×10−72.55\times10^{-7}2.55×10−7 on the full panel; within-bucket medians rise from 4.25×10−104.25\times10^{-10}4.25×10−10 (1–5 sweeps, n=8,886) to 1.75×10−91.75\times10^{-9}1.75×10−9 (21–50 sweeps, n=181). The Pearson correlation between the two per-case medians and log⁡10\log_{10}log10​ of the within-case maximum alpha difference is 0.22 on GWAS and 0.26 on eQTL. The trend is weak: regrouped operations accumulate slowly with sweep count, and most of the case-to-case spread comes from how closely Q\mathbf QQ and the active components are conditioned.

Both panels keep every converged-pair comparison below 3×10−73\times10^{-7}3×10−7. The two recorded eQTL exceedances only appear when both backends stop at max_iter=100 without convergence; their maximum absolute alpha differences (1.59×10−51.59\times10^{-5}1.59×10−5 and 4.35×10−54.35\times10^{-5}4.35×10−5) are the two highest points on the eQTL panel. The user acceptance applies to this both-nonconverged condition only; exceedances involving a converged backend remain gate failures.

The per-case records retain the backend-specific iteration counts, convergence flags and alpha errors used in the figure; the longer iteration-limit diagnostic revisits the eQTL exceedances with max_iter=1000.

Independent toy example

The packaged toy example checks numerical agreement on a single simulated dataset, using a separate susieR reference. Load its summary statistics and LD with prusie.load_example().

The independent 500-variant toy example converged in 3 iterations and returned 2 credible sets. Its maximum absolute PIP difference from the separately generated susieR 0.16.6 reference was 1.66e-10.

The dashed lines mark the simulated nonzero effects. Credible-set memberships also match the R reference for this example. See the toy example tutorial for the simulation method, fitting options and packaged reference, and the vector PDF.

Reproduce the comparisons

The benchmark summary and full per-case records provide numerical errors and convergence diagnostics. The 24 adopted earlier-protocol cases retain their saved final arrays from retained repeat seven; other cases save the arrays from retained repeat three. These repeat identities are explicit. Follow the regeneration instructions to rebuild the tables and figures from retained measurements without refitting.

The offline toy checks compare a prusie fit with the packaged R reference. The fresh susieR comparison fits both implementations using matched inputs and settings. Repeating the real-data comparisons requires the separately obtained association summaries and LD, with the same variant order and fitting settings. Preserve the frozen inputs and reference files when running new checks.

Longer iteration-limit diagnostic

The separate local workstation supplement selected all 224 GTEx genes for which both backends had reported nonconvergence at max_iter=100. Each backend was called once per gene with max_iter=1000, with every other setting and the original local inputs unchanged. This diagnostic does not change the default, formal benchmark settings, acceptance criterion or original classification.

Both backends converged for 102 genes and returned nonconverged fits for 83. For the other 39 genes, both stopped with the existing prior-variance guard; those calls did not return fits and have no final iteration count. There were no discordant backend outcomes. The guard message suggests inconsistent summary statistics and LD as one possible cause; this supplement did not establish that cause.

Among the 185 returned pairs, the largest absolute alpha difference was 5.49×10−85.49 \times 10^{-8}5.49×10−8; among the 102 both-converged pairs, it was 2.59×10−82.59 \times 10^{-8}2.59×10−8. For ENSG00000110203.10, both still reported nonconvergence after 1,000 iterations, with maximum alpha difference 7.16×10−107.16 \times 10^{-10}7.16×10−10. This does not replace the original 100-iteration exceedance or its recorded user disposition.

The supplement records list every gene, returned iteration count, numerical difference or guard rejection. The selected gene list has SHA-256 8ee34a2840903be09fa500241d646e508b1ddbd217f7996f07c59caadd125e47.
